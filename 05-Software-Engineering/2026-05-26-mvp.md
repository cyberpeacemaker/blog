A **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)** is the earliest, most basic version of a new product that still delivers enough value to be usable by early customers.

Popularized by Eric Ries in his book *The Lean Startup*, the goal of an MVP is to test a business hypothesis and collect the maximum amount of validated learning about customers with the least amount of effort and cost.

---

### The "Viable" in MVP

A common misconception is that an MVP is a broken, half-finished, or low-quality product. In reality, "viable" means the product must actually solve the user's core problem, even if it lacks advanced features or a polished design.

> **The Skateboard Analogy:** If your goal is to build a vehicle to get a customer from point A to point B, you don't build a single tire (not viable). You build a skateboard (MVP). It’s basic, but it works. Later, based on feedback, it evolves into a scooter, a bicycle, a motorcycle, and eventually a car.

---

### Why Build an MVP?

* **Validates Market Demand:** It answers the crucial question: *Do people actually want this?* before you spend months or years building it.
* **Saves Time and Money:** By developing only core features, you minimize the risk of wasting resources on a product that nobody will buy.
* **Generates Early Revenue:** A functional MVP can start generating income or attracting investors to fund further development.
* **Establishes a Feedback Loop:** Early adopters will tell you exactly what features they want next, guiding your product roadmap with real user data rather than assumptions.

---

### MVP vs. Final Product

| Feature | Minimum Viable Product (MVP) | Final/Mature Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Feature Set** | Only the essential core features | Comprehensive feature set |
| **Development Time** | Weeks or a few months | Months to years |
| **Primary Goal** | Learning, validating, and testing | Scaling, revenue maximization, and retention |
| **Target Audience** | Early adopters and beta testers | The mainstream market |
| **Investment** | Low to moderate | High |

### Famous MVP Examples

* **Zappos:** Founder Nick Swinmurn didn't build an elaborate inventory system. He went to local shoe stores, took photos of shoes, and put them online. When someone bought a pair, he went back to the store, bought the shoes at full price, and shipped them. This validated that people were willing to buy shoes online.
* **Dropbox:** Before building complex file-syncing infrastructure, the founder created a simple, 3-minute explainer video demonstrating how the software *would* work. The video drove thousands of sign-ups overnight, proving the demand.
* **Airbnb:** The founders couldn't afford rent, so they put three air mattresses in their living room and created a basic website offering "Air Bed and Breakfast" to attendees of a local design conference. It proved that people were willing to pay to sleep in a stranger's home.
