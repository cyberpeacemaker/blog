The formal **DARPA AI Cyber Challenge (AIxCC)** was a two-year, $29.5 million tournament that officially **concluded in August 2025** at DEF CON 33 (where Team Atlanta took first place).

Because the primary tournament is over, **active registration for the live prize competition is currently closed**. However, because the core goal of the challenge was to secure critical infrastructure at scale, the ecosystem remains highly active.

## How to "Participate" in the AIxCC Ecosystem Now

Even though you cannot enter the original competition, DARPA, ARPA-H, and their open-source partners have made everything publicly accessible so the broader cybersecurity community can build on top of what was created.

### 1. Build on the Open-Source Archive

All seven finalist teams were required to release their **Cyber Reasoning Systems (CRSs)** as open-source software.

- You can access the official competition frameworks, telemetry data, challenges, and the source code of the winning AI models directly via the [AI Cyber Challenge Official Website](https://aicyberchallenge.com/).
    
- This allows you to deploy, test, and adapt these autonomous, LLM-driven vulnerability discovery and patching systems for your own projects.
    

### 2. Join Community Standardization Efforts (OpenSSF)

The Linux Foundation and the **Open Source Security Foundation (OpenSSF)** were foundational advisors for the challenge. Following the competition, winning teams have partnered with OpenSSF to develop a **unified framework and standard** to advance the future of Cyber Reasoning Systems. You can get involved with OpenSSF working groups to contribute to these post-AIxCC standards.

### 3. Attend Hands-On Tutorials & Labs

The techniques pioneered during AIxCC are being taught at major security conferences and academic tutorials. Top teams regularly host hands-on labs focusing on:

- Fuzzing harness input generation via LLMs.
    
- Automated prompt engineering for agentic CRSs.
    
- Compiler overrides and static analysis tools paired with AI.
    

## How the Original Competition Worked

If DARPA or ARPA-H launches a spiritual successor or a new phase of the challenge, the original blueprint will likely serve as the model:

- **The Tracks:** Competitors could join via the **Funded/Small Business Track** (which awarded initial $1 million grants to small businesses to fund innovation) or the **Open Track** (where independent teams registered by submitting a technical white paper).
    
- **The Goal:** Teams had to build an entirely autonomous system (a CRS). During the competition rounds, human interaction was strictly prohibited.
    
- **The Rules:** Systems were given raw open-source code (primarily in C and Java). The AI had to automatically find vulnerabilities, generate a Proof of Vulnerability (PoV), and write a functional patch without breaking the intended software features.
    

## Watching for Future DARPA Challenges

DARPA frequently launches new prize challenges and Broad Agency Announcements (BAAs) in the AI and security space. To catch the next tournament early, keep tabs on:

- The **DARPA Information Innovation Office (I2O)** homepage.
    
- The official **DARPA Opportunities** page or SAM.gov.
