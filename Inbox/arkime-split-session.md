You hit the nail on the head with your observation. While they appear as three separate rows in Arkime, **they are actually segments of the exact same network connection.**

Here is exactly how to interpret what you are seeing and why Arkime split them up.

### 1. Why do they have the same Community ID?

The **Community ID** is a standardized string generated by hashing the network "5-tuple":

- **Source IP:** `192.168.65.69`
    
- **Source Port:** `54,583`
    
- **Destination IP:** `10.99.40.20`
    
- **Destination Port:** `80`
    
- **Protocol:** `TCP`
    

Because all three entries share these exact same five attributes, they generate the exact same Community ID hash (`1:H7SmQHE5G0S8ba68UmYpXtbhdM=`). This tells you that they belong to a single, continuous conversation between that client and server.

### 2. Why did Arkime create three separate rows?

Look closely at the **Packets** column for each row:

- **Row 1:** 10,000 packets
    
- **Row 2:** 10,000 packets
    
- **Row 3:** 6,795 packets
    

Arkime has built-in safety thresholds to prevent individual session logs from becoming too massive to handle efficiently. By default, **Arkime splits a single continuous flow into a new log entry every time it hits 10,000 packets** (or hits a specific time/byte threshold).

Because this connection was heavy and incredibly fast, Arkime filled up two full 10,000-packet buckets and put the remaining 6,795 packets into a third bucket.

### 3. How to Interpret the Timeline & Action

If you piece these three segments back together chronologically, you can reconstruct the full story:

- **The Action:** A client using `curl/7.85.0` initiated an HTTP `POST` request to upload something to the endpoint `/file/upload`.
    
- **The Volume:** The entire transfer consisted of **26,795 packets** in total.
    
- **The Speed:** Look at the timestamps. The whole event started at `23:39:01` and ended at `23:39:02`. This massive burst of traffic happened in a span of just **1 to 2 seconds**.
    
- **The Flow Breakdown:**
    
    - **Row 1 (The Start):** Captures the initial TCP handshake, the HTTP header data (`POST /file/upload`), and the first 10,000 packets of the upload.
        
    - **Row 2 (The Middle):** Captures the next 10,000 packets of the ongoing data stream.
        
    - **Row 3 (The End):** Captures the final 6,795 packets and the closing of the connection.
        

> **Summary:** This is a single, large, high-speed file upload via `curl` that Arkime sliced into three pieces for log-management purposes.
